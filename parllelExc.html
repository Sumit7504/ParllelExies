Bicycle Racing

Road bicycle racing is the cycle sport discipline of road cycling, held primarily on paved roads. Road racing is the most popular professional form of bicycle racing, in terms of numbers of competitors, events and spectators. The two most common competition formats are mass start events, where riders start simultaneously (though sometimes with a handicap) and race to a set finish point; and time trials, where individual riders or teams race a course alone against the clock. Stage races or "tours" take multiple days, and consist of several mass-start or time-trial stages ridden consecutively.

Paragliding

Paragliding is a recreational and competitive adventure sport of flying paragliders. It involves the use of a paraglider, where the pilot sits in a harness which is suspended by a large, curved fabric wing. There is no engine, pilots launch by running and obtaining lift. The curve in the wing enables a skilled pilot to glide over long distances and to climb to high altitudes. Paragliding is often seen as one of the more accessible extreme sports.

Surfing

Surfing is a surface water sport where individuals, known as surfers, use a board to ride on the face of moving waves, typically towards the shore. The sport has its roots in Hawaii and Polynesia, where it was practiced by people of all social strata. Today, surfing encompasses various styles and techniques, and it is celebrated globally with events and competitions, such as those organized by the World Surf League, which provides news, live events, and athlete rankings. Surfers can catch ocean, river, or man-made waves, gliding across the water until the waves break.
